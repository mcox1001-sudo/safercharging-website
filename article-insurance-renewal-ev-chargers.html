EV chargers and your insurance renewal: get ahead of the question

A flag you cannot answer at renewal is an awkward, expensive surprise. The time to sort it is before the assessor asks.

Safer Charging• 27 May 2026• 5 min read

The short version

EV chargers are showing up more often in insurance renewals and fire risk assessments. A charger-related flag you cannot answer at renewal is an expensive surprise. Getting an EV-specific inspection done — and any faults put right, with insurer-ready reporting — before the assessor asks gives you a clean picture and time to fix things quietly, rather than scrambling against someone else's deadline.

Insurance renewals used to ignore EV chargers. They do not any more. Increasingly, insurers and fire risk assessors are asking pointed questions about the chargers on a site — and a flag you cannot answer at renewal is an awkward, expensive surprise at exactly the wrong moment.

The good news is that this is one of the most manageable risks on your estate, provided you deal with it on your timetable instead of someone else’s.

Why chargers are getting flagged now

Two things are happening at once. EV chargers have become common enough that assessors can no longer treat them as a novelty, and there have been enough poorly installed estates out there that the industry has learned to look harder. The result is that the humble car-park charger is now a line item in fire risk assessments and renewal questionnaires.

For managing agents and property owners in particular, that means charge points have quietly become one more compliance obligation — and most suppliers will inspect but not fix, so the risk lands straight back on your desk.

The problem with waiting for the assessor

If the first time you look hard at your chargers is when the assessor raises them, you have lost your best option: time. A flag at renewal forces you to react. You are now trying to arrange an inspection, understand the findings, source remediation and produce evidence — all against a deadline set by someone else, often with cover riding on it.

The time to get charge points inspected and put right is before the assessor asks, not after.

An honest look now does the opposite. It gives you a clean picture, and the breathing room to fix anything quietly and properly rather than scrambling.

What ‘handled’ looks like

Charge point compliance does not have to live on your desk. Done properly, one specialist owns the whole thing:

  • The inspection — an electrical inspection and fire risk assessment specific to EV charging, not a generic once-over.
  • The fix — remediation of what is found, so you are not left holding a report and a problem.
  • The evidence — clear, insurer-ready reporting you can hand straight to your insurer or assessor.

That is the difference between a report that flags a problem and a partner who resolves it. You can read more about how we approach EV charge point fire risk and compliance, but the principle is simple: inspection and remediation belong together.

A sensible timeline

If you know roughly when your fire risk assessment or insurance renewal falls, work back from it. Getting an audit and any inspection done well ahead of that date means findings can be remediated calmly, and you walk into the review with evidence in hand rather than questions to answer.

The estates that sail through renewal are not lucky. They looked early. A Charge Point Audit is the cheapest way to buy yourself that head start.

Key takeaways

  • EV chargers are increasingly flagged in fire risk assessments and insurance renewals.
  • A flag you cannot answer at renewal is a deadline-driven, expensive surprise.
  • Inspection alone is not enough — you need the remediation and insurer-ready reporting too.
  • Auditing ahead of your renewal date buys time to fix things quietly and properly.

Frequently asked

Do insurers ask about EV chargers at renewal?

Increasingly, yes. Insurers and fire risk assessors now commonly flag EV chargers. Having an EV-specific inspection and any remediation done in advance, with insurer-ready reporting, means you can answer confidently.

What reporting do I need for EV charger compliance?

Reporting written to satisfy insurers and compliance requirements, covering the EV-specific electrical inspection, fire risk assessment and any remediation carried out. Safer Charging provides insurer-ready reporting as standard.

How far ahead of my renewal should I get EV chargers checked?

Far enough that any findings can be remediated calmly rather than against a deadline. Working back from your fire risk assessment or renewal date and starting with an audit is the safest approach.
